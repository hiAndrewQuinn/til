title: LLMs are driving a return to linguistic prescriptivism
date: 2026-08-24
tags: 
- against-entropy
---


[Lingusitic prescriptivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_prescription)
has been out of vogue for some time now. 

When you imagine
the proverbial school teacher exchange,

    You: Can I use the bathroom?
    
    Teacher: I don't know. *Can* you?

    You: (groan) *May* I use the bathroom?

you are dealing with a prescriptivist in miniature.
And[^1] for good reason: it's easy to take someone speaking
[a dialect of English you may not hear on the pickleball courts often](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African-American_Vernacular_English)
and use *your* ignorance of its rules as a lever to
dehumanize. 

But what if the actual source of lingusitic
drift isn't a human at all? What if it's simply the emergent
property of a fantastically addictive technology sucking
up the hearts and minds of impressionable youths everywhere,
quietly bending everything it prints in their native
language to be *just a bit* more like English, or maybe
Mandarin Chinese?

This thought crosses my mind every time I see an exchange
on some language-learning subreddit where native speakers
of the language decry the output of the machine as riddled
with errors. That is probably true given their own standards.
But communication did successfully occur in most of these
cases between the human subject actually conversing with the
LLM. You often hear classist apologetics deployed
as well: "Oh, the 20 dollar a month digital Aristotle
couldn't possibly give you a *real* taste of the language.
What you need is to employ a tutor, who likely costs closer
to 20 dollars an *hour*. Poor silly poor. Don't you know
that's the only way to acquire *true* fluency?"

Aesthetically speaking I get why the invasion of the
machine god at the end of history changing the very words
your children will speak and how they speak them is 
horrifying. 
I played *Metal Gear Solid V* too, man.
But surely a native speaker smart enough to
spot the anomaly in the robospeak also... well, understand
the robospeak. And when we think of the untold extillions
of dollars of drade lost, 
and extillions of hours burned in pursuit of secondary
language acquisition the world over, I don't really think
we are in any position to start griping that the migrant
who learned competent A2 level LLM-flavored English / French / 
[Icelandic](https://openai.com/index/government-of-iceland/)
in two years instead of three just didn't try hard enough.
You understand them. They understand you. Their kids will
also likely acquire your L1 faster on the margin a 
decade or two from now, and they'll probably master it to an
even deeper level on average than people a generation before
them, because they have an infinitely patient, infinitely
erudite Pokedex for everything in their pocket who doesn't
mind that they've been yapping for 200,000 words already about
the different kinds of clouds they see in the sky or what
the difference between Greek and Turkish yogurt *really* is.
